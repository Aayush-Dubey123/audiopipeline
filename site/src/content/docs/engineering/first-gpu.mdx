## Lesson 1: keep the model loaded

The first version ran each chunk in a fresh worker process. That is clean and isolated, but it **reloaded the whole model for every chunk**. Moving to a single resident model, plus a speaker-conditioning cache keyed by the reference’s content hash, turned model setup from a per-chunk cost into a once-per-session cost.

## Lesson 2: “faster” settings can be slower

Mixed precision (fp16/bf16) is the textbook speed-up. I added it behind a setting and benchmarked it on the same chunks and seeds before turning it on:

<Aside type="tip">
	On small laptop GPUs the token-by-token generation loop is dominated by overheads that casting makes worse. **Measure on your own hardware before trusting a rule of thumb.**
</Aside>

## Lesson 3: memory is a budget, not a limit

	<Card title="One job at a time" icon="list-format">

	</Card>
	<Card title="Release once, not always" icon="random">
		Clearing the CUDA cache after every chunk slowed things down; once per job is enough.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Unload when idle" icon="moon">
		After 5 idle minutes the model is unloaded, so the laptop is usable for other work.

## Lesson 4: the CPU does most of the work

Only text-to-speech generation touches the GPU. Cleaning the reference, checking chunks, joining, loudness and encoding are all fast CPU/NumPy/FFmpeg work. Keeping them off the GPU is a big part of why a 6 GB card is enough.
